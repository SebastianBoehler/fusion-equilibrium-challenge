### 1.1 Award selection — a correction to the handoff
The handoff says each award takes the *best* scored submission. The forum brief (2 and 5 Oct

- **Numerator:** the MAST S of the *selected* submission. By default this is the **latest
  successfully scored** Final submission. A different one can be nominated by email to
  `fusionec26@sophelio.io` before the close.
- **Denominator:** the best DIII-D S across all scored Final submissions.

**Consequences:**
- **Today the default for both awards is submission 2**, the one with the wrong solenoid.
  Uploading submission 3 changes the default to 3.
- Without an upload, only a nomination email avoids submission 2.
- A worse submission 3 is **not** protected by a "best of" rule. Choosing between 1 and 3 is
  a blind decision for the human.
- The challenge paper (arXiv 2609.01750) reportedly says "highest-scoring entry is forwarded",
  which contradicts the forum. If in doubt, ask in the nomination email.

### 1.2 The score
S = 0.55·R²ψ + 0.15·mean(R²q95, R²βN) + 0.10·(1 − D_LCFS) + 0.20·mean_j max(0, R²_j).

- Each consistency R²_j is **pooled over all frames**.
- A frame where our LCFS extraction fails is **mean-substituted**.
- The official scorer passes the MAST envelope as `mask_coarse` / `mask_f`, for the O-point
  search and the LCFS bisection, on **both** the label ψ and ours. We may not use it.

## 3. Why shape lags while flux is close (mechanism)

### 3.1 R²ψ cannot see shape
- SS_tot of ψ is dominated by the external flux (solenoid swing, PF coils). The plasma's
  near-boundary structure is a tiny share of it.
- **DIII-D proxy evidence** (`runs/mast-020-d3d-proxy.md`, `runs/mast-022-gs-picard.md`):
  - Exact external field + oracle circular Gaussian: R²ψ **0.985**, consistency 0.36, κ R² −6.5.
  - Exact external field + oracle axis R + our kinetic GS: consistency 0.78, but **κ 0.55
    and l_i 0.33**.
- So even with a perfect field model, J ∝ Te^p caps κ and l_i well below 0.9.

### 3.2 New: κ, tri and l_i are set by modelling choices, not by the data
- **Setup:** the scorer's own functionals on 2,204 public frames (every 8th shot, every 4th
  frame). Our vessel region was the mask. Submission 3 (ALLgf, Te^0.75) vs 030 (no passive,
  Te¹), each treated as the other's "truth".
- **Script:** `research/review_scalar_spread.py`.

| Scalar | SD (sub3) | R² (030 vs sub3) | Mean shift 030 − sub3 (SD) | Top 2% of frames' share of SS_res |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| R_axis | 0.057 m | 0.89 | −0.01 | 0.80 |
| Z_axis | 0.053 m | 0.74 | −0.04 | 0.91 |
| κ | 0.195 | **0.15** | **−0.58** | 0.26 |
| tri_top | 0.078 | 0.60 | +0.19 | 0.53 |
| tri_bot | 0.057 | **0.30** | +0.23 | 0.56 |
| volume | 1.09 m³ | 0.72 | −0.02 | 0.60 |
| l_i | 0.156 | **−0.86** | **+1.15** | 0.11 |

**Reading:**
1. **Two of our own physically plausible configurations disagree on κ, tri_bot and l_i about
   as much as 030 disagrees with the labels.**
   - These scalars are set by the unconstrained choices (passive currents, current peaking).
     The release inputs do not pin them.
   - Submission 3 raises κ by about 0.11 and lowers l_i by about 0.18 relative to 030. Its
     scored κ and l_i will move a lot, and the direction is unknown.
   - **Speculation:** the direction is plausible. The outboard B_Z probes favour submission 3
     (0.08 → 0.73 on holdout), and published MAST values (κ about 1.9–2.0, l_i about
     0.85–1.0) are closer to submission 3. These values are context only, never used for
     fitting.
2. **R_axis, Z_axis, volume and tri are outlier-dominated.**
   - 2% of frames carry 53–91% of the disagreement. These are shot-level vertical-class and
     topology decisions.
   - Pooled R² punishes a few wrong shots, not small per-frame noise.
3. **κ and tri are read at the X-points.**
   - Zmax and Zmin of the LCFS sit at or near the X-points, where |∇ψ| → 0, so δx = δψ/|∇ψ|
     diverges.
   - Near connected double null, a few mm of drsep decide which X-point bounds the scorer's
     lowest closed contour. EFIT++'s own drsep is uncertain by several mm (NSTX/MAST XP418).
4. **l_i has no data handle in the release.**
   - Externally, only Λ = βp + l_i/2 is measured, and separately only for elongated plasmas
     with magnetics.
   - The labels come from EFIT++ with a low-order Lao polynomial basis, zero edge current and
     magnetics plus a Dα LCFS constraint. The basis is published for MAST-U; MAST-era orders
     are unverified.
   - Our l_i comes from Te^0.75. Te carries little information on l_i in an unrelaxed MAST
     plasma (τ_R ≳ the shot length).
   - A pure bias of 1 SD costs the full l_i term. Our two configurations differ by 1.15 SD.

families that could move κ and l_i all need per-frame information that the release does not
contain. In the allowed space that information exists only in FAIR-MAST magnetics. We used
it to *select* configurations but not to *learn* per-frame corrections. That is the one
unexplored axis.

## 5. Can we close it ourselves?

### 5.1 Oracle ceiling for per-frame profile choice informed by magnetics (predeclared)
- **Tuning set:** 80 shots.
- **Method:** for each frame, the ALLgf profile variant (Te^0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0 / 1.5, edge
  0.1 / 0.2) with the smallest plasma flux-loop residual is picked **in sample**.
- **Gate:** if the oracle gains < 0.02 plasma-loop R² over the base, no learned per-frame
  profile model can pass the replacement gate.
- **Result** (`research/review_oracle_profile.py`; 7,365 frames):

  | Variant | Plasma loops CC* | Plasma loops coils |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Base (ALLgf, Te^0.75) | 0.565 | 0.773 |
  | Best single variant | 0.568 (edge 0.1) | 0.778 (edge 0.2) |
  | **Per-frame oracle (in sample)** | **0.569 (+0.004)** | **0.817 (+0.044)** |

  \*CC values sit below the documented 0.786 because this script excludes broken channels by
  plasma-part R² rather than the harness's total-flux R². The coil column reproduces the
  harness exactly (0.773).

  The oracle spreads its choices fairly evenly across all six variants (742–1,557 frames
  each), so no single variant dominates.

- **Reading:**
  - The oracle is an upper bound: it is chosen in sample, frame by frame, with the targets.
    It gains nothing on the centre column. Its +0.044 on the coil loops is inflated by
    selection noise.
  - A regressor from release inputs would recover only part of it. Earlier rules recovered
    none: Ip-ramp and Thomson-edge peaking gave ≤ 0.01.
  - **Gate: fail.** Per-frame profile choice cannot credibly beat submission 3 by 0.02 on
    both loop groups. Profile peaking is not where the magnetics residual lives; the
    remaining residual must sit in the external field, passive structure or position.
